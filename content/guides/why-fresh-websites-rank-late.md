Every new site owner asks the same question in week three: "why does Google not love me yet?" The honest answer is a system working as designed — and knowing how it works tells you exactly where effort pays.

## The real timeline of a new domain

| Phase | What Google is doing | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Discovering URLs via sitemap and links | A few pages indexed, then "Discovered — currently not indexed" for many |
| Weeks 3–8 | Crawling, testing rankings, measuring signals | Bouncing positions (page 9 → page 4 → page 7), tiny impressions |
| Months 3–6 | Trust consolidating for pages that earned it | Stable positions, impressions compounding |
| Month 6+ | The site competes "for real" | Ranking becomes a function of content quality and links |

The bounce in week 3–8 is normal — Google tests new pages in real searches, measures how searchers react, and adjusts. It is not a penalty; it is measurement.

## "Discovered, currently not indexed" — the badge of every young site

This Search Console status means exactly what it says: Google knows the page exists and has decided indexing it is not urgent. On a 3-week-old domain with no external signals, that decision is rational — Google indexes the web on a budget, and trust is allocated by evidence: content quality, links from other sites, user behavior, history.

**What moves pages out of the queue:** time, content depth, internal links from pages that already rank, and eventually external links. Nothing you can paste into a form changes it overnight — including repeated "Request indexing" clicks (a tool for new/changed URLs, not a general accelerator).

## The four things that genuinely help

1. **Depth per page.** A page that answers the query fully — with examples, numbers, tables — earns longer visits and return visits. Thin pages stall in the queue; rich pages graduate.
2. **A coherent site.** Pages that reference each other (tools ↔ guides) build topical authority — Google learns what the site is *about*.
3. **Patience with publishing cadence.** Steady output (weekly) beats a burst then silence: crawling follows a schedule you train.
4. **One or two real links.** A mention in a forum, a partner page, a directory in your niche — earlier than you think, this matters more than another 50 pages.

## What does NOT help (common myths)

- ❌ Submitting to "search engines" (there are two that matter; both find you via sitemap)
- ❌ Keyword-stuffing meta tags — [write them for humans](/tools/developer-tools/meta-tag-generator)
- ❌ Buying links / link farms (short-term gain, long-term penalty)
- ❌ Rebuilding the site every month (resets trust signals)
- ❌ Checking positions hourly (the bounce will hurt your morale more than your rankings)

## The mental model that keeps you sane

Google is a slow investor doing due diligence. Every page is a pitch; every month of consistent quality is a payment history. At some point the relationship compounds — the same effort starts returning more — and pages you published in month 1 start ranking in month 6 without extra work.

Build for the visitor, measure weekly (not hourly), and let the compounding do its quiet work.
