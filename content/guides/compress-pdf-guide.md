"How much smaller will my PDF get?" — the honest answer is **it depends on what is inside the file**. But after compressing thousands of documents, clear patterns emerge. Here are realistic expectations so you know whether your result is normal — and what to do when it is not.

## What makes a PDF heavy

A PDF is heavy for one of three reasons:

1. **Scanned pages** — every page is a full-resolution photo. A 20-page scan can easily pass 30 MB. This is where compression shines.
2. **High-resolution photos** — export from design tools often embed images at 300+ DPI at full size.
3. **Embedded fonts and duplicate resources** — usually a minor factor (a few hundred KB).

Text-only PDFs (contracts, invoices generated by software) are already tiny — a few KB per page. Compressing them yields almost nothing, and that is normal.

## Realistic compression numbers

| Document type | Typical before | Typical after (quality ~medium) | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scanned document (300 DPI) | 20–40 MB | 3–8 MB | **70–85%** |
| Photo-heavy export (InDesign/Canva) | 10–25 MB | 2–6 MB | **60–80%** |
| Mixed report (charts + photos) | 5–10 MB | 2–4 MB | **40–65%** |
| Text-only with embedded fonts | 0.3–1 MB | ~same | **0–15%** |
| PDF that is already compressed | any | same | **~0%** |

Two important truths:

- **You cannot compress what is already compressed.** If someone ran the file through a compressor before, round two gives nothing.
- **Quality settings matter**: aggressive quality (e.g., 40–50%) shrinks more but makes images visibly soft. 70–80% is the sweet spot for documents that must remain readable when printed.

## What to do when compression is not enough

If your file is still too big for the upload limit or email:

1. **Split it.** Send chapters separately with the [PDF Split](/tools/pdf-tools/pdf-split) tool — useful when only one section is needed by the recipient.
2. **Remove pages you do not need.** Draft pages, blank pages, appendixes — cut them before compressing.
3. **Rebuild image-heavy pages.** If the source is yours (Word, Canva), re-export images at lower resolution, or rebuild the PDF from optimized images with [Images to PDF](/tools/pdf-tools/images-to-pdf).
4. **Merge later.** Split → compress each part → [Merge](/tools/pdf-tools/pdf-merge) back. Extra work, but sometimes crosses a hard size limit.

## How to compress safely

The mechanism matters for confidential documents: a good compressor either **uploads your PDF to a server** (privacy risk, and slow for big files) or **rewrites the file locally in your browser**. Our [PDF Compressor](/tools/pdf-tools/pdf-compress) runs 100% client-side — the file never leaves your device, which is exactly what you want for contracts, medical records and financial documents.

Quick checklist before you compress:

- [ ] Work on a **copy** (keep the original for printing)
- [ ] Try quality ~75 first; go lower only if still too big
- [ ] Check the result at 100% zoom — is the text still sharp?
- [ ] Still too big? Split or remove pages instead of pushing quality below ~50%

## Bottom line

Scans and photo-heavy files shrink 60–85%. Text PDFs barely shrink — that is the format working as designed, not a failure. Set expectations by content type, keep quality at 70–80%, and when the limit is brutal, split instead of degrading.
