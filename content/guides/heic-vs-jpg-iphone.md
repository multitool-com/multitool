Buried in iPhone Settings there is a camera choice most people never open: **Settings → Camera → Formats**. Two options — *High Efficiency* (HEIC) and *Most Compatible* (JPG) — and the wrong pick for your workflow costs either storage or convenience. Here is how to decide.

## What each setting does

- **High Efficiency** — new photos save as **HEIC**. Same visual quality, roughly **half the storage** of JPG.
- **Most Compatible** — new photos save as **JPG**. Bigger files, but open on virtually anything.

## The storage difference, in real numbers

| | HEIC | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| Typical iPhone photo | ~1.5 MB | ~3 MB |
| 1,000 photos | ~1.5 GB | ~3 GB |
| 10,000 photos (avg library) | ~15 GB | ~30 GB |

On a 128 GB phone, a few years of photography is the difference between "plenty of space" and constant storage anxiety. That is why Apple defaults to HEIC.

## The compatibility tax

HEIC stumbles in specific places:

- Older Windows versions and some legacy software
- Certain website upload forms (CMS, banking, government portals)
- Some printers and photo labs
- Occasionally in email attachments to recipients on old systems

Meanwhile **Apple converts silently in many contexts** — AirDrop to non-Apple devices, some email apps, most social apps — which is why problems appear "randomly".

## Which should YOU use?

**Keep High Efficiency (the default) if:**
- You mostly share via WhatsApp/Instagram/AirDrop (they handle conversion);
- storage matters to you (any phone under 256 GB);
- you keep your library in Apple Photos.

**Switch to Most Compatible if:**
- You constantly export photos to Windows/older systems and hate converting;
- you upload frequently to a picky portal that rejects HEIC;
- storage is not a concern.

**Our recommendation for most people:** stay on High Efficiency and convert on demand — the next section makes that painless.

## Quality: does HEIC lose anything?

Both HEIC and JPG are lossy, but HEIC is a newer, smarter codec — at the same file size HEIC looks **better**, not worse. Converting HEIC → JPG at 90%+ quality is visually lossless for screens and normal prints. Rule: convert from the original once; avoid converting a file back and forth repeatedly.
